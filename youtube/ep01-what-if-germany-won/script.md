# What If Germany Had Won World War II?

Script for the English YouTube channel, episode 1. This is an original script on the same
topic as the Kok Bisa video, not a translation of it. It is written for a global audience.

- Target length: about 5 minutes (~720 words at ~145 words per minute)
- Tone: curious and calm. Do not joke about the victims. Avoid swastikas in the thumbnail and
  close-ups so the video stays advertiser-friendly.
- `[VISUAL]` lines are storyboard notes and are not read aloud.

---

## 1. Hook (0:00–0:30)

[VISUAL] A history book opens. Three flags join hands like a gang. A map of Europe fills with arrows.

World War Two was the deadliest conflict in human history. It started when three countries,
Germany, Italy and Japan, teamed up around one extreme idea: that they deserved to rule the world.
They invaded their neighbors, the Allies fought back, and the rest is history. Germany and its
friends lost.

[VISUAL] The book slams shut. It reopens with the pages glitching and the ending rewritten.

But what if they hadn't? What would your history books say today? What would the world look like,
and what would your life look like, if this man and his allies had won?

## 2. Two fatal mistakes (0:30–1:10)

[VISUAL] A chessboard. Two German pieces make reckless moves.

First, we need to be fair: most historians think a German victory was very unlikely. But Germany
made two decisions that made losing almost certain.

Mistake number one: in June 1941, Germany invaded the Soviet Union, a country so huge that its
army simply could not supply its troops across it. Then winter arrived, and German soldiers were
still in summer uniforms.

[VISUAL] Freezing soldiers. A supply line on the map stretches thinner and thinner until it snaps.

Mistake number two: in December 1941, just days after Japan attacked Pearl Harbor, Germany
declared war on the United States, the biggest industrial power on Earth.

[VISUAL] American factories pour out tanks, planes and ships like a fountain.

Luckily for us, Germany did both. So let's imagine a world where it did neither.

## 3. Europe under the Reich (1:10–2:40)

[VISUAL] A tall pyramid of people. A few figures sit at the top; the bottom fades into darkness.

Nazi ideology sorted human beings into a ranking by "race". At the top were the so-called Aryans,
meaning Germans and closely related peoples. Below them came other Europeans. At the very bottom
were Jews, Roma and Slavic peoples such as Poles and Russians. Gay people and people with
disabilities were also treated as having no place at all.

[VISUAL] The music drops out. The screen goes quiet and dark.

In the real war this belief had already led to the Holocaust, in which six million Jews were
murdered. A victory would not have ended it. It would have finished it.

[VISUAL] A sealed folder labeled "Generalplan Ost". It opens to show a map of Eastern Europe with
arrows pushing people east.

Victory would also have unlocked a secret plan called Generalplan Ost, or "Master Plan East".
Tens of millions of people in Poland, Ukraine, Belarus and Russia would have been expelled,
starved or worked to death. The people who survived would have become slave labor, and their land
would have been handed to German settlers.

[VISUAL] A wheelchair stands alone in an empty hospital corridor.

The cruelty didn't stop at borders. Under a program called Aktion T4, the regime had already
killed about 70,000 disabled people in its first official phase alone, many of them German
citizens. The goal was a "perfect" population. In a world where Germany won, that program would
have had no reason to stop.

## 4. A world divided (2:40–3:30)

[VISUAL] A globe spins. Three colors spread across it.

So what about the rest of the world? In 1962 the novelist Philip K. Dick imagined exactly this in
The Man in the High Castle. In his version, Germany controls Europe, Italy takes the
Mediterranean and North Africa, and Japan rules Asia and the Pacific.

[VISUAL] The United States splits into three parts: a German east, a Japanese west and a nervous
buffer zone in the middle.

Even America gets carved up. The east goes to Germany, the west coast goes to Japan, and a thin
neutral strip is squeezed in between.

It's fiction, but it's built on the Axis powers' own ambitions.

## 5. What about Asia? (3:30–4:30)

[VISUAL] A map of Asia with a banner reading "Greater East Asia Co-Prosperity Sphere". The word
"Prosperity" cracks.

Japan called its empire the "Greater East Asia Co-Prosperity Sphere". It promised to free Asia
from Western colonizers. In reality it was just a new colonizer, and often a far more brutal one.

[VISUAL] Prisoners and laborers lay track through the jungle under armed guards.

Across Southeast Asia, from Burma to Indonesia, hundreds of thousands of people were forced into
labor. On the Burma–Thailand "Death Railway" alone, more than 100,000 workers and prisoners of
war died.

[VISUAL] A family register. Korean names are crossed out and replaced with Japanese ones.

In occupied lands, schools taught Japanese, and people had to bow toward the Emperor in Tokyo.
In Korea, families were even pressured to give up their names and take Japanese ones. If Japan
had won, that would have been permanent. Hundreds of millions of people would have lived under
someone else's language, someone else's names, and someone else's rules.

[VISUAL] A world map of colonies. The wave of independence movements after 1945 freezes and
fades away.

And the wave of independence that swept Asia after 1945, in countries like Korea, the
Philippines, Indonesia, Vietnam and India? Much of it might never have happened.

## 6. Ending (4:30–5:00)

[VISUAL] The dark map slowly returns to today's colorful world.

That's the world we might have lived in: an iron fist over Europe, a brutal empire over Asia, and
freedom as something people could only dream about.

Fortunately, it's only a "what if". The real outcome was paid for by millions of people who
fought, and many of whom died, so that this version of history would stay fiction.

[VISUAL] The mascot looks at the camera.

What else do you think would have changed if the Axis had won? Let us know in the comments.
And if you have a question you've always wondered about, drop it below. We might answer it next.
Thanks for watching. Stay curious.

## Fact-check notes (verify before recording)

- Operation Barbarossa: invasion of the USSR on 22 June 1941.
- Germany declared war on the USA on 11 December 1941, four days after Pearl Harbor.
- Holocaust: about 6 million Jews murdered.
- Generalplan Ost: plans for the deportation, starvation and killing of tens of millions of Slavs.
- Aktion T4: about 70,000 people killed in the official phase (1939 to August 1941); killings
  continued afterwards.
- The Man in the High Castle (Philip K. Dick, 1962): the map of the US divided into three is
  taken from this novel.
- Forced labor in Southeast Asia (romusha): estimates range from hundreds of thousands to
  millions of people. "Hundreds of thousands" is the safe figure.
- Burma–Thailand Railway: roughly 90,000+ Asian laborers and ~12,000 Allied POWs died.
- Sōshi-kaimei (Korea, 1940): policy of forcing Koreans to adopt Japanese-style names.

## Changes from the Kok Bisa version

- The script is rewritten from scratch rather than translated, so it is not a copy of someone
  else's content.
- Jokes about the victims ("om kumis kotak", "Terima kasih Google Translate") were removed. A
  global audience and YouTube's advertiser rules are stricter about Nazi topics.
- Concrete facts were added (dates, Generalplan Ost, Aktion T4) to build credibility.
- The writer behind the "divided world" scenario is named (Philip K. Dick).
- All Indonesia-specific references ("our country", "where we're from") were removed. The Asia
  section now covers the whole region so viewers anywhere can relate to it.
